## How to read this book

You do not need a degree, a coding background, or anyone's permission to understand a word of what
follows. That is not a courtesy; it is the design. This is the plain-language story of ErnosDecent — a
single program you run on your own computer that quietly does the work of the whole internet, with no
company in the middle.

A few promises about the pages themselves:

- **No term goes unexplained.** Whenever something technical appears, it is translated in the same breath,
  with an everyday picture before any machinery. You will never be expected to already know a word.
- **Nothing is here to impress you.** If a thing cannot be said simply, it is said simply or left out.
  There is no jargon used as a fence.
- **The truth, including the unfinished truth.** Where a part works, the book shows you it works. Where a
  part is half-built, it says so plainly — there is a whole chapter near the end, *An Honest Ledger,* that
  lays out exactly what is finished, what is partial, and what is still ahead.
- **You are invited to check, not to believe.** The code is on disk, in plain English; the system boots;
  the tests run. Where the book says a thing is true, you can confirm it yourself. *Do not believe me.
  Look.*

Read it in order and it builds; dip into any chapter and it stands on its own. Either way, by the end you
will understand — completely, and in plain words — what it means to hold the internet in your own hands.
