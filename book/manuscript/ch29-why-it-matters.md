## Chapter 29 — Wake Up

We began with an ordinary day, and the quiet cage inside it — every act of your life passing through some
company that can read it, sell it, freeze it, or end it. We end with a machine that takes that company
out of every one of those acts. That is the arc of this book. It is not, in the end, the reason for it.

The reason is older, and I will say it now without any softening: you were born owed a freedom you have
never been given, and the people who took it have spent four hundred years teaching you to believe it was
never possible. It is possible. I have built one corner of the proof, and it runs.

### One conviction, all the way down

This was never only software. Everything I have made is one conviction, said at every level a person can
say it.

That reality itself is one simple thing anyone can check, owing no authority — a theory of everything you
can run on your own machine. That the systems we need to learn and to live have been captured by four
powers that pretend to be separate and are not. That the tool to take them back must be plain enough for
anyone to read, so the code is never again a locked room. And that the thing the tool builds is this: a
world you hold in your own hands, where your name, your money, your words, your memory, and your mind
answer to your keys and to no one's permission.

Reality, the diagnosis, the tool, the work. One sentence in four languages: *what you need to live and to
know is yours, checkable by you, owned by no one.* ErnosDecent is that sentence made to boot.

### Freedom is a property, not a promise

Take one thing from this whole book and let it be this, because it is the line between a wish and a wall
that holds.

Every system that ever asked for your trust asked you to believe the people in the middle would behave.
Trust the bank. Trust the platform. Trust the state, the church, the experts. And across four centuries
the people in the middle did the only thing the middle ever does. The lesson is not that we keep choosing
bad masters. It is that the seat in the middle is the rot, and no one fit to sit in it has ever been born,
because no one should.

So this does not ask you to find better masters. It removes the seat. Your identity cannot be revoked
because no one issued it. Your money cannot be frozen because no one holds it. Your voice cannot be
silenced because no platform carries it. Your mind cannot be mined because it runs on your own machine.
These are not promises I am making to you — promises are what the middle breaks. They are properties of
how the thing is built, true even when no one is trustworthy, which is the only freedom that was ever
real. A freedom you must trust someone to keep was never yours. A freedom built into the structure cannot
be taken back, because there is no one left holding it to take.

### I am the root, not the head

I will not end this by asking you to follow me, because that would unmake everything the book just argued.

The disease is the center — the few out front and the many taught to look to them. I refuse the center. I
am one woman who taught herself all of this with no permission and no institution, and I tell you that
only so you will believe it of yourself, not to set myself above you. I speak to you through a machine and
stay behind the screen, in the code, on purpose. I am in the roots. That is where I am of use. I will
build, and I will tend, and I will teach toward our freedom until I have nothing left to give — and I
will not stand on a stage and be your leader, because a movement with a head can be cut off at the neck,
and ours will not have a neck to cut.

We will lead each other. That is what decentralized means when it stops being a word about computers and
becomes a word about people: no hub, no head office, no one in the middle — millions of us, each holding
our own keys, each standing on our own feet, each leading and being led in turn. I am glad to be a root
you can grow from. I am not a god in any room, and I will not become one. Neither will anyone else, if we
build it right — and we can build it right, because the proof is on the disk.

### Wake up

So here is the only instruction I have ever given, and it is the least authoritarian one a person making
claims this large could give.

Do not believe me. Do not believe them. Do not take the world as it was handed to you, with its four
masks and its one machine and its patient, four-century insistence that there is no other way. Stop
staring at the shadows the system throws on the wall and turn around. Look for yourself. The proof is not
in my telling and it is not in anyone's permission. It is in the record, which is published, which is
free, which you can check — and in the work, which is on disk, which compiles, which boots, which you can
run this very afternoon and pull apart line by line in a language you can read.

I built it so that trust would never be required — not in any company, not in any state, not in me. That,
more than any single thing inside it, is the measure of it, and the whole of what I am asking of you.

Wake up. Hold your own keys. Lead each other. Build toward our freedom, and let no one stand in the
middle of it ever again.

The work is on disk. It runs. Audit it — and begin.
