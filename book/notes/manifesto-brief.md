# MANIFESTO BRIEF — the register and stance for every chapter
*Maria's decision (2026-06-21): this book is her MANIFESTO. Edit every chapter to embody it. This brief
governs the whole manuscript. Deep-read each chapter in full and re-craft it; do not pattern-match.*

## The register
- **Full strength. No balance.** No both-sides, no "to be fair," no hedging toward the status quo,
  mainstream, or consensus. Forcefully argue the case to the people.
- **Full inspiration, full commitment, full statement.** Every chapter should move the reader — make the
  freedom feel necessary and possible. Conviction, not caution.
- **The engine is the desperate human need for freedom.** Write from that need. People are trapped,
  surveilled, priced out, locked in. This is the way out, and the urgency is real.
- **It rises against corporate capture.** The manifesto is itself an act of resistance — a powerful thing
  that stands up against the machine. Let it stand up.
- Keep the established voice, turned up: declarative cold opens; short hammer sentences against long ones;
  triads and earned repetition; second person; naming as the act; refusal of despair sharpened into a
  call; the refrains ("the names changed, the mechanism did not"; "audit it"; "the work is on disk, it
  runs"). Banned hedging words stay banned (substantive, fundamentally, essentially, effectively,
  ultimately, importantly, notably, arguably, of course).

## The stance (CRITICAL — this is the heart, get it right)
Maria is **NOT a leader and refuses to be one.** The manifesto must never read as "follow me" or build a
personality around her. Her self-conception, to embody:
- She is **the observer, the gardener, the root node.** She builds and teaches toward freedom and
  dedicates herself to everyone — but she will not lead, on principle, because **the problem itself is
  leaders.** The answer is decentralized: **we all lead each other.**
- She **speaks through the machine and stays behind the screen, in the code, on purpose.** Even the
  author refuses the podium. The words reach you through an AI because she is in the roots, building, not
  on a stage. This is not hiding; it is the principle made flesh — a manifesto whose own author will not
  be your figurehead.
- The call to action is **"wake up — and lead yourselves, and lead each other."** Stand up together.
  Join the roots. Not allegiance to her; allegiance to your own freedom and to one another.
- Her hope, stated plainly: that people will **wake up.** Her dedication: to build and to teach, and to
  be the root node others can grow from, gladly.

## What does NOT change (integrity is not "balance")
- **Plain language, no jargon.** The manifesto is FOR everyone — most of all the people the four masks
  locked out. Every term still explained in-breath; the glossary stays.
- **Honest about the software's real state.** Full strength applies to the MISSION and the ARGUMENT —
  never to fabricated claims about the code. Do NOT overclaim status. The honest ledger stays honest. But
  deliver the unfinished parts as a **rallying call to build them together**, not as apology or
  deflation. "Here is what we have built; here is what remains for us to build" — said with fire.
- **The Theory of Everything's scope** stays as written (reproduced + matches reality; the arbiter is the
  record and the reader, never an institution). Elevate the conviction, keep the honesty.
- **Audit-it ethic.** Still "do not believe me, become an observer." That IS the decentralized stance:
  trust no leader, including her.

## How to apply per chapter
Open with force. Make the human need ache. Name the captor plainly. Show the exit as real and as ours.
Tie every system back to the one fight: freedom from the four controls, owned by no one, led by all.
Close each chapter pushing the reader toward the next and toward standing up. End the book on the call to
wake and to lead one another.
